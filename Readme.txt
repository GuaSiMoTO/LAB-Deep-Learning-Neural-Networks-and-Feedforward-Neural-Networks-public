==========================================
Background
==========================================

Bike sharing systems are a new generation of traditional bike rentals where the entire process from membership, rental, and return has become automatic. Through these systems, users can easily rent a bike from one location and return it to another. There are over 500 bike-sharing programs globally, with more than 500,000 bicycles in operation. These systems are gaining interest due to their role in addressing traffic, environmental, and health issues.

Apart from real-world applications, the data generated by these systems offers attractive research opportunities. Unlike other transport services (bus, subway), bike-sharing systems explicitly record trip duration, departure, and arrival locations, transforming these systems into virtual sensor networks for monitoring mobility in the city. This data can potentially help detect significant city events by analyzing usage patterns.

==========================================
Data Set
==========================================

The bike-sharing rental process is highly influenced by environmental and seasonal factors. Variables like weather conditions, precipitation, day of the week, and season affect rental behavior. The core dataset contains two years of historical data (2011 and 2012) from the **Capital Bikeshare system** in Washington, D.C., USA, publicly available at [Capital Bikeshare](http://capitalbikeshare.com/system-data). Data is aggregated on an hourly and daily basis, with corresponding weather and seasonal information extracted from [Freemeteo](http://www.freemeteo.com).

==========================================
Associated tasks
==========================================

- **Regression**:  
  Predict bike rental counts on an hourly or daily basis using environmental and seasonal factors.

- **Event and Anomaly Detection**:  
  Bike rental counts are correlated with events in the city, traceable via search engines. For instance, querying “2012-10-30 Washington D.C.” on Google reveals results related to Hurricane Sandy. Major events are identified in [1], making the dataset useful for validating anomaly or event detection algorithms.

==========================================
Files
==========================================

- **Readme.txt**  
- **hour.csv**: Aggregated bike sharing counts on an hourly basis (17,379 hours).  
- **day.csv**: Aggregated bike sharing counts on a daily basis (731 days).

==========================================
Dataset characteristics
==========================================

Both `hour.csv` and `day.csv` share the following fields, except `hr` (not available in `day.csv`):

- **instant**: Record index  
- **dteday**: Date  
- **season**: Season (1: spring, 2: summer, 3: fall, 4: winter)  
- **yr**: Year (0: 2011, 1: 2012)  
- **mnth**: Month (1 to 12)  
- **hr**: Hour (0 to 23)  
- **holiday**: Whether the day is a holiday (from [holiday schedule](http://dchr.dc.gov/page/holiday-schedule))  
- **weekday**: Day of the week  
- **workingday**: 1 if the day is neither a weekend nor a holiday, 0 otherwise  
- **weathersit**: 
  - 1: Clear, few clouds, partly cloudy  
  - 2: Mist + cloudy, broken clouds, mist + few clouds  
  - 3: Light snow, light rain + thunderstorm, scattered clouds  
  - 4: Heavy rain, ice pellets, thunderstorms, snow, fog  
- **temp**: Normalized temperature in Celsius (divided by 41)  
- **atemp**: Normalized “feeling” temperature in Celsius (divided by 50)  
- **hum**: Normalized humidity (divided by 100)  
- **windspeed**: Normalized wind speed (divided by 67)  
- **casual**: Count of casual users  
- **registered**: Count of registered users  
- **cnt**: Total bike rentals (casual + registered)
